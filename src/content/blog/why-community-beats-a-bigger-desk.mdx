Walk into any coworking sales pitch and you'll get a tour of furniture: ergonomic chairs, standing desks, phone booths, the "premium" floor. All fine. All missing the point.

Nobody's business ever changed because their desk got 20cm wider. Businesses change because of a conversation — the client referral, the collaborator, the person who's already solved the problem you're stuck on. The most valuable square footage in any workspace is the space between people.

## The desk-farm illusion

Big coworking chains sell scale as a feature: hundreds of members, multiple floors, an app. The unspoken promise is that more people means more networking.

In practice, the opposite happens. Past a certain size, a room full of strangers behaves like a railway station — everyone's polite, nobody connects. "Community" gets outsourced to a notice board and a WhatsApp group with 400 muted members. You are, functionally, alone — just in a more expensive chair.

## What actually creates connection

Sociology has known this forever, and anyone who's hosted a good dinner knows it too:

- **Size.** Real familiarity has a ceiling. In a small room, you know everyone by week two. In a big one, you never will.
- **Curation.** A room works when someone thought about who's in it. Random assembly produces a crowd; selection produces a community.
- **A host.** Introductions don't happen by osmosis. Someone has to say *"you two should talk — you're solving the same problem."*
- **Repetition.** Trust compounds through small daily contact: the same faces, the chai run, the "how did the pitch go?"

None of these scale. That's not a bug — it's the whole mechanism.

## The compounding math

Say a curated room of thirty gets you three genuinely useful connections in your first quarter — a collaborator, a referral, a mentor. Each of those knows thirty more people. That's how independent careers actually grow: not by broadcasting to a crowd, but through warm paths between small rooms.

A bigger desk depreciates. A network compounds.

## What to look for instead

When you tour a coworking space, ignore the furniture and ask:

1. **"How many members do you have?"** — and whether the answer sounds like a community or a stadium.
2. **"Who would you introduce me to?"** — a real community has an instant, specific answer.
3. **Watch the room.** Are people talking to each other, or is it rows of headphones?
4. **Ask how people join.** If there's no conversation before a membership, there's no curation after it.
