Working from home is free, quiet-ish, and zero commute. A coworking space in Jaipur costs real money. So why do people pay?

Having built a space on one side of this argument, we'll try to be honest about both.

## Where home wins

- **Cost.** Nothing beats free.
- **Commute.** The kitchen is very close to the bedroom.
- **Deep solitude.** For some kinds of heads-down work, an empty flat is genuinely great.

If your work is fully solo, your discipline is iron, and you never need to meet anyone — home is hard to argue with.

## Where home quietly loses

The costs of home-working show up late and compound:

- **The loneliness tax.** Independent work is isolating. Weeks can pass without a real professional conversation, and motivation erodes in ways that are hard to notice from inside.
- **The credibility problem.** Client call over a barking dog; nowhere to host a meeting; a home address on the invoice. None of it is fatal — all of it chips away.
- **The boundary collapse.** When home is the office, the workday never really ends, and never really begins either.
- **The network you don't build.** Nobody ever got introduced to a collaborator by their ceiling fan. Opportunity comes from proximity to other people building things.

## What coworking actually buys

Not the desk — the desk is the least of it:

1. **A boundary.** Leaving the house tells your brain the day has started. It's embarrassingly effective.
2. **Infrastructure that isn't your problem.** Wi-Fi, power backup, coffee, a meeting room when a client shows up.
3. **Ambient accountability.** Working around people who are working is a productivity drug with no side effects.
4. **A network on tap.** The person two desks over knows the vendor, the client, the shortcut you need.

## The catch: not all coworking delivers this

Here's the part the brochures skip — a big anonymous desk-farm gives you points 1 and 2, and quietly fails at 3 and 4. If "community" means a WhatsApp group nobody uses, you've paid for a desk with extra steps.

The things that actually fix isolation — being known by name, real introductions, events small enough to talk at — only happen in rooms deliberately kept small. ## How to decide

- Try a **week of each**. Feelings beat spreadsheets here.
- Count the **conversations**, not the hours. Which week moved your work forward?
- If you go coworking, **tour at 11am on a weekday** — feel the real room, not the sales pitch.
