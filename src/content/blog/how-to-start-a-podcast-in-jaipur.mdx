So you want to start a podcast in Jaipur. Good news: it has never been easier to get a show live. Better news: most of what stops people — gear, acoustics, editing — is solvable without buying anything.

Here's the honest, practical path.

## 1. Decide the format before the gear

Most first-time podcasters shop for microphones before they know what they're making. Flip it:

- **Solo or guests?** A solo show needs one clean audio chain. Interviews need two or more, plus a room that doesn't echo.
- **Audio-only or video?** Video podcasts get discovered on YouTube and Instagram, but they double the setup: lighting, framing, backdrop.
- **Episode length and cadence?** A 20-minute weekly show is far more sustainable than a 90-minute "whenever" one. Consistency beats production value.

Write down: format, length, cadence, and the one listener you're making it for. Everything else follows from that.

## 2. The home-setup trap

You can record at home — many people start there. But be honest about what home recording in an Indian city sounds like: traffic, the ceiling fan, an empty room's echo, a neighbour's renovation. Listeners forgive a lot; they don't forgive bad audio.

Fixing that at home means a decent dynamic mic, an audio interface, acoustic panels, and hours of trial and error. It adds up — in money, and in the energy you wanted to spend on the actual show.

## 4. Your first episode, step by step

1. **Script the skeleton, not the sentences.** Bullet-point your intro, three segments, and an outro. Reading a script sounds like reading a script.
2. **Record a two-minute test.** Listen back on earphones. Fix levels and seating distance *before* the real take.
3. **Record the real thing.** Aim for done, not perfect — episode one is for learning the rhythm.
4. **Edit lightly.** Cut the dead air and the false starts. Resist re-recording; ship it.
5. **Publish everywhere via one host.** A podcast host distributes your feed to Spotify, Apple and the rest automatically.

## 5. Keep going (this is the real skill)

Most podcasts die by episode seven. The antidotes:

- **Batch-record** so a busy fortnight doesn't kill the streak.
- **Guest-swap** with other creators — every guest brings their audience.
- **Work near other people who publish.** It's much harder to quietly quit your show when the desk next to you asks how episode twelve went.
